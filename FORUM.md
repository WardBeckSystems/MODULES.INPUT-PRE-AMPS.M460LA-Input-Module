> Hello.
>
> Spoke with Dad again last night regarding the M460 series. I am trading a few of my M460A units for M460LA modules, and I remembered one of Tony's questions;
>
> The M460 was a super deluxe module, full EQ, and assignment. CBC Vancouver commissioned WBS to outfit its new studios - way back when. They wanted a slightly stripped down EQ and that ended up being the M460A. Over the years many other customers wanted a change here or there - either in EQ, assignment, both or other things, and they became the M460B, C, D, E, F, G, etc... The M460L was a nice module with the full 3 band (like the M460) but with a tweak for NBC. Then NBC wanted to buy 4 (I believe) consoles with a slightly different module. On the fold back they wanted 1 extra assignment on the existing switch. So basically this switch went from a 6 pole to an 8 pole (or 4 - 6 he forgets). This was a tiny change so it was decided to not make a new module designation just make it an M460LA thus reads module 460 model L version A. As it turns out (completely by fluke) they were intended for the Los Angeles Olympics.
>
> So there you go the evolution of the M460LA module.
>
> And as a side note YES there was a 4 band version of the M460 module. But it never went in to production as it was more economical to to a module with the assignment, and have another module up under the meter bridge with the EQ.
>
> I have some M460LA modules at his house in a box and a few more on the way. I will later this year be able to give you a little more of an accurate description of the difference (wether it is a 4 or 6 or 8 contact shadow switch) or what. When he and I get together again to re-furbish them so I can use them in my new studio set-up!! And I will try and post photographs on Tony's main site illustrating this.
>
> Regards,

>
> The M460 series of input modules had numerous subscripts running from M460 to M460M. Because of the application of M460 modules to almost 100% custom consoles, if WBS were able to use an exsisting M460 design, they did. If not, they knocked off a new M460 and gave it the next available subscript letter. M460LA is a coincidence. Another was the M460C - used for consoles provided to CBS-TV.
>
> As the company grew...
>
> Ron and Roger eyes were always on every project. Obviously every customer did not require a custom design, particularly as the company grew and "standard" console designs were implemented - M1203, M1204, L2042, L3242, R1000, R1200, R2000, and T1202. WBS had a Systems Engineering Dept., which grew to about 10 people, to implement small to moderate customization using existing modular elements. The company reached 110 people at it's peak, in two buildings, at 841 Progress Ave., Scarborough.

> Tony has a couple of manuals here for the M460 series. I use them to maintain all my M460 modules. THey are all essentially the same except for additional routing switches, and terminology - Fold Back, PA, Reverb Send, etc... the evil module is the M - of which I have 4. They were made for 1 customer and there were 2 large boards supplied. The boards made it to Dan Alexander this year and he parted them out. They were engineered not by Dad but the engineering department of building 1. They are a nightmare, very different internal wiring from the rest of the M460 series and Dad was not amused with that evolution, so much so that he waded back in and designed and directed every project form that point on except for the Renaissance consoles - he had absolutely nothing to do with those with the exception of suggesting the control room volume pot be an infinite rotation optical type pot. The M460M is a 3 band fully parametric EQ with an additional daughter amplifyer board jury-rigged in place to provide a line level feed to a FOH board. The boards were initially used for game shows, then 1 went off to do sound track work and the FOH mod was used to go to a smaller board for headphone mixes and producers feeds. 
> The stupidity of the M460M (OK I am rambling and wayyyy off topic) is the wiring of the Pendar switch (CH ON thing) it was not done to standard convention, which was done to save locating (1) Shadow switch to the extrusion, and floating it off the mother PCB (like the CUE on an M490 module)!!!

## 460a distortion

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jan 28, 2013|46_

> Dad designed all modules to work off a fully regulated bi-polar powersupply. The modules further regulated the voltage, making the voltage that was used internally to the module as perfectly clean as it could possibly be. So some bright bulb pulled the voltage regulator from your module by the looks of things (closer detailed pictures would assist my speculation). The module in a console would work well for quite some time, with virtually no noticeable detriment to its performance. BUT when we rip apart a console, and rack modules to use in our smaller studios we often give the modules not necessarily the best power. IT can oscillate, be under rated a bit, the balance between the two rails might be out of synch, etc.. These imbalances can cause component failure, as Dad's regulator could cope with such imbalances and keep things in order inside the module (case in point of the 4 M460La modules I just refurbished for a client). So I strongly recommend going to the electronic supply haus in your neighbourhood, buy a pair of regulators - TIP29 and TIP30 (any suffix will be acceptable we sued B's), and re-build your missing regulator. IF you need a bit of help here with this project PLEASE ASK. THere are a few VERY IMPORTANT things of which one should be aware. You will need 4 other regulators as well, a couple of diodes, and some insulators for the TIP regulators - we mount those to the module's frame as your module is a very early 1970's variant.
